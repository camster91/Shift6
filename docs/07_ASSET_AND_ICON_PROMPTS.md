# SHIFT6 — Image, Illustration & Custom Icon Production Plan

All assets must be original. The supplied Dribbble concept is mood/reference only.

## Asset families

1. SHIFT6 logo and app icon
2. Navigation/system icons
3. Workout-category icons
4. Equipment icons
5. Exercise instructional media
6. Muscle maps
7. Pastel 3D accent objects
8. Empty-state illustrations
9. App Store / Play Store screenshots
10. Social/share cards

## A. Logo exploration prompt

> Create an original identity exploration for a modern fitness brand named SHIFT6. The core concept is measurable progress in six-week training cycles. Develop a clean geometric wordmark and an S6 monogram. Explore a numeral 6 built from a continuous progress loop, six subtle segments, or a forward-shifting notch. Premium, approachable, gender-neutral, calm, not aggressive. Avoid lightning bolts, flames, skulls, generic heartbeat marks, and copied fitness logos. Use near-black, white, soft lavender and light blue. Flat vector presentation on a clean light background. Show wordmark, monogram, small-size mark, and one-colour version.

## B. App icon prompt

> Design an original mobile app icon for SHIFT6, a six-week fitness progression app. Use a minimal S6 or six-segment progress-loop symbol, bold enough to read at 32 px. Near-black base with a lavender-to-blue accent, no text, no tiny detail, no gradients that reduce accessibility, premium iOS/Android product feel, original geometry, centred, strong silhouette.

## C. 3D category-object prompt

> Create a set of original soft 3D fitness category objects for a premium mobile app. Rounded toy-like geometry, matte materials, gentle studio lighting, isolated on transparent or pure light background, no text, no logos. Objects: barbell plates, dumbbell, six-segment progress ring, bike wheel, balance disc, resistance band loop, foam mobility block, stopwatch. Palette: lavender, powder blue, mint, warm yellow, charcoal. Consistent camera angle and scale. Playful but sophisticated, suitable as small dashboard card accents.

## D. Equipment icon-system prompt

> Design a cohesive set of 24 px outline icons for a mobile fitness app, with a matching filled selected state. Rounded 2 px stroke, optical consistency, minimal detail, black primary colour, legible at small size. Include: barbell, rack, bench, plate, dumbbell, kettlebell, cable machine, resistance band, pull-up bar, dip bars, medicine ball, landmine, treadmill, stationary bike, rower, elliptical, sled, box, mat, foam roller, machine stack, bodyweight/person, watch, heart/health connection. Original icon shapes; no copied icon library styling.

## E. Workout category icon prompt

> Create an original icon family for Strength, Hypertrophy, Cardio, Mobility, Recovery, Power, Balance, Core, Running, Cycling and Custom Program. Simple geometric symbols, rounded outline style, unmistakable at 24 px, matching SHIFT6 design language. Provide outline, filled, and monochrome variants.

## F. Exercise-photo prompt template

> Instructional fitness exercise image for a mobile app: [EXERCISE NAME]. Show an adult athlete demonstrating the correct [START/MID/END] position with [EQUIPMENT]. Full relevant joints and equipment visible, realistic but moderate training load, neutral modern gym, uncluttered background, natural proportions, accurate biomechanics, no text, no brand logos, landscape 4:3 crop with safe centre framing for mobile cards. Inclusive professional fitness photography, even lighting, educational rather than dramatic.

Generate multiple frames only if they can remain mechanically consistent. Human-reviewed technique is mandatory before use.

## G. Exercise-loop/video production brief

Preferred long-term method is filmed or carefully animated demonstrations, not uncontrolled generative video.

Per exercise:
- 3–8 second silent loop;
- front/side angle chosen by movement;
- neutral tempo;
- setup visible;
- start/end clear;
- no extreme load;
- captions separate from video;
- human technique review.

## H. Muscle-map prompt

> Create a clean original anatomical muscle-map illustration for a fitness app. Front and back human body, neutral athletic proportions, simplified educational anatomy, no explicit detail, primary muscle highlighted in lavender, secondary muscles in light blue, all other anatomy light grey, white background, vector-like flat shading, accessible and clinical-neutral rather than bodybuilding-poster style.

## I. Empty-state illustration prompt

> Minimal original mobile-app illustration for the state “[STATE]”. Use simple rounded 3D geometry and subtle fitness objects, lavender/blue/mint palette, generous negative space, calm premium tone, no text, no people unless necessary, designed to sit above a short headline and button on a white screen.

States:
- no workouts yet;
- no records yet;
- offline but workout available;
- no health connection;
- cycle complete;
- custom program empty.

## J. App Store screenshot art direction

Sequence:
1. “Progress, six weeks at a time” — Home + cycle progress.
2. “Your plan, your equipment” — program selection/builder.
3. “Log every set, even offline” — active workout.
4. “A coach that explains the why” — coach proposal.
5. “See what changed in six weeks” — cycle review.
6. “Hundreds of exercises. Build anything.” — exercise library.

Visual rules:
- use actual product UI;
- no fabricated metrics presented as real user claims;
- light lavender environment;
- large accessible headline;
- consistent phone framing.

## K. Figma asset workflow

1. Produce exploration board.
2. Approve style.
3. Build vector master where possible.
4. Name assets semantically.
5. Export SVG for icons, PNG/WebP/AVIF for raster where supported, MP4/H.264 or platform-appropriate video for exercise media.
6. Test at real mobile sizes.
7. Store source attribution/licence metadata.
8. Never merge generated human exercise media without technique review.
